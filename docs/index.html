How much power will your panels give next hour?

SolarCast forecasts the next hour of solar power for any spot in New Zealand and any panel size, from a 5 kW home to a 50 kW business, so you can run the EV charger, hot water or machines on your own sunshine instead of the grid.

How it works

Our own machine-learning model makes every forecast. It only uses what is known at the end of the current hour, and it was tested once, on 16 months it had never seen.

1

Weather in

Hourly sunlight, cloud, temperature and wind from Open-Meteo, plus its day-ahead forecast for the next hour (issued 24 hours earlier, so no peeking at the future).

2

Forecast

Two gradient-boosted tree models, one for sunlight and one for cloudiness (the clear-sky index), averaged. Trained 2022-2024, tuned on early 2025.

3

Your panels

Sunlight becomes power for the panel size you choose (3 to 100 kW): next hour in kW, the day in kWh and what it is worth. Worked example: a workshop charges its EV when solar covers the load and the car.

See where the power goes

When you pick a spot on the dashboard, an animated picture shows the next hour: sunlight hits your panels and the power flows into your home. The more of their rated power the panels give, the brighter the sun and the faster the power moves. When the worked example says "charge now", the power flows on to the EV. At night the home runs on the grid.

Charge your EV from your panels. Pick your car from about 40 EVs and plug-in hybrids sold in New Zealand (type a brand or model to find it: Tesla Model 3 and Y in every version, the older Model S and X, BYD including the Sealion 5, Sealion 6 and Shark 6 plug-in hybrids, MG, Kia, Hyundai, Polestar, Volvo, Toyota, Nissan Leaf), the battery level now and the target. Add a home battery such as a Tesla Powerwall 3 to see how much left-over sun it stores for the evening, for the car and the home.

Results

Test period July 2025 to October 2026 (16 months the model never saw), daytime hours, all 14 regions. Average error of the next-hour forecast, lower is better. Output scales with panel size, so the error as a share of capacity is the same for a 5 kW home and a 50 kW business.

Method50 kW business5 kW home% of capacity
Persistence (same as last hour)4.03 kW0.40 kW8.1%
Open-Meteo day-ahead forecast alone1.88 kW0.19 kW3.8%
Clear-sky persistence1.66 kW0.17 kW3.3%
SolarCast v1 (weather so far)1.19 kW0.12 kW2.4%
SolarCast v4 (current)1.12 kW0.11 kW2.2%

v4 is 32.6% better than clear-sky persistence, 40.3% better than Open-Meteo's own day-ahead forecast, and beats both baselines in every region (14/14). The same error is 7.9% of the average daytime output. Weather is modelled (reanalysis) and PV output is derived with a stated formula, because New Zealand has no public hourly data for single solar sites; real plants would be harder to forecast.

Can it get better? We tried (v5, judged on the validation period only, with a bar set in advance: at least 1 W/m² better than v4): inputs for how wrong the day-ahead forecast is right now, the forecast's trend, and five tuning variants. The best gained only 0.26 W/m² (28.98 → 28.71), so v4 stays and the test period was not looked at again. Then v6 used the GPU: XGBoost trained on a Colab T4, 48 settings plus a blend with v4. Best 28.56 W/m² (gain 0.42), still below the bar. The error left seems to come from the weather data itself, not the model.

Worked example: better forecast, better decision

The same charging rule, driven by different forecasts on 6,818 workshop-days of the test period, settled with the actual sunshine. Grid power the EV causes per day (lower is better).

StrategyGrid kWh / day

Clear-sky persistence forecast7.60

Perfect forecast (upper bound)5.31

Even against the good 10:00-14:00 habit, SolarCast saves 1.5 kWh a day per workshop; a perfect forecast would add only 1.65 kWh more.

AI you can trust with the numbers

On top of the forecast, an open language model (Qwen3) runs on the notebook's own machine. It writes a national briefing every hour and answers questions in English or Chinese, but it never computes or invents numbers.

  • Grounded: the server looks up the facts with the same code as the map; the model only explains them.
  • Offline: no internet, no tools, no API keys.
  • Guarded: keyword and injection filters, the Llama Guard 3 safety classifier, topic rules and rate limits.
  • Size-aware: every number is already scaled to your panel size before the model sees it. Tested on a T4: a 5 kW home in Christchurch gets 3.36 kW next hour, the 50 kW workshop example 33.6 kW.
  • Honest: in replay mode it says the hour is in the past, and the page always shows which answers came from the AI.

On your own computer

Python 3.10 or newer. Open http://127.0.0.1:8000 when it is ready.

git clone https://github.com/nashwu0117/SolarCast-Lite.git
cd SolarCast-Lite
python -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e .
python -m solarcast.nz

Optional AI: install Ollama, run ollama pull qwen3:1.7b, then python -m solarcast.nz --ai.
